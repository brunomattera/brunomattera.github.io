Overview

I work as an extension of Oluko's product team: I own design across the Context experience, run research and workshops with leadership and school experts, and turn fuzzy requirements into screens, prototypes and decisions the team can build on.

Oluko is an education company building New Insights, a platform that captures how learners grow across social, emotional and learning skills, and shows it to the people who support them. My work spans the learner side (short daily check-ins) and the adult side (the tools teachers, interventionists and principals use to act on what learners share).

The challenge

Schools already collect plenty of data. What they lack is a calm, trustworthy way to notice a learner who needs more support, decide what to do, and know if it's working, without turning a child's wellbeing into a compliance report. On top of that, the product had grown fast: new features, mixed language between screens and a design system that needed to keep up.

The Client

Oluko & New Insights

A research-backed product that looks at the whole learner, built for the adults who see them every day.

New Insights comes from years of research on how children develop social and emotional skills. Oluko is turning that research into software schools can use at scale: learners take short, friendly activities, and educators get a clear picture of how each one is doing and what might help.

The product has two lines. Core goes deep on skills through guided routes. Context, where most of my work lives, focuses on the day to day: how learners feel, what kind of support they're asking for, and how the classroom responds.

Spotlight · Tier 2

From a fuzzy idea to a testable prototype

Tier 2 started as a sentence in a roadmap. Most of the work was getting a room full of experts (product, leadership, former educators, sales) to agree on what the real problem was before anyone drew a screen.

01

Ran a requirements session to list what schools already do for Tier 2, who does it, and where the product could help without adding work.

02

03

Turned the shared journey into early concepts for each role, kept rough on purpose so feedback stayed on the flow, not the pixels.

04

Prototype

Built a clickable HTML prototype that matches Figma, with a guided tour so stakeholders can review it on their own time.

05

Test with schools

Next up: short one-to-one sessions with teachers and a principal at a partner school, then a new iteration with what we learn.

A Key Reframe

Types of support, not moods

Early sketches showed each learner's "vibe" as high or low. That was wrong. In New Insights, what a learner shares maps to MOVE: four kinds of support they're asking for. Reframing the dashboard around MOVE changed every chart, from good/bad scores to a weekly grid of what each learner needs.

Design Decisions

What shaped the concepts

Every decision came from the workshop, the product requirements or an expert in school support systems. A few of the ones that changed the design the most:

01

Add to the classroom view, don't replace it

Teachers already know the current learners table and tabs. Tier 2 sits on top of that view as a focused section, so nobody has to learn a new place to work.

02

People flag first, data later

Automatic flags need validated cut scores that don't exist yet. So the first version lets educators flag a learner themselves, and leaves room for data-driven flags when they're ready.

03

Progress against a goal line

Following how school teams actually work, each plan has an end goal and a line from where the learner starts. Above the line means it's working; below means adjust. Simple enough to read in a meeting.

04

Every decision has a reason

Continue, adjust, refer to the team or return to Tier 1: each option is a clear card, and a short "why" is required. Together with a tier history, it gives the next person a record they can trust.

Reflection

What this project is teaching me

This is ongoing work, and the next chapter starts with real schools. A few things are already clear.

01

Workshops beat documents

One well-prepared hour with the right people settled questions that weeks of comments couldn't. The board itself became the brief.

02

Get the model right before the UI

The MOVE reframe and "tiers are additive" changed more screens than any visual tweak. Asking experts early saved whole rounds of redesign.

03

Prototypes travel further

A clickable prototype with a guided tour let busy stakeholders review on their own time, and it turned out to be useful for sales demos too.
